PriorityIndependent observationResponse / decision still open
1 - anatomyGrape leaves need a basal sinus and major veins radiating from the petiole; the earlier pointed, elongated shapes were not convincing.Rebuilt as broader serrated, palmate leaves. Most leaves remain unfolded as recognition anchors.
2 - anatomyWhite-oak lobes were too angular, and acorn cups looked too deep.Replaced with seven rounded fingerlike lobes and deep sinuses; flattened the scaly cups and added short peduncles.
3 - growth clarityFruit and foliage overlapped without enough readable attachment routes.Grapes, oak, juniper and hops now have connected, largely concealed support stems appropriate to each plant. Barley and rye have individual culms; acorn stalks and woody fruit twigs are connected.
4 - line hierarchyDense hatch knots and repeated curled edges competed for attention.Reduced wine hatching and limited folds to selected leaves. Further simplification elsewhere remains a discussion, followed by actual-size and scrap tests.
5 - subjective rhythmThe wine and oak sections were denser than the long arm; similarly upright corn motifs repeated conspicuously.Added small rye fans around the retained grain-head placements. Corn now has two different angles, raised kernel crowns, shaded side faces and partially peeled husks. Oak fruit angles and foreground leaf overlap are deliberately varied.
6 - subjective transitionsSome changes between plant sections read abruptly rather than as gentle handoffs.Added a smaller late grape bunch, a small hop leaf/cone among the first juniper sprays, tapered terminal juniper needles, early/late rye heads and smaller introductory oak leaves. Four transition zooms show the actual interleaving; plants do not share a fictitious common vine.

Strengths noted: generous clear tabletop space and coherent edge-following placement; curved hatching already gives useful volume through lines alone.

Next decisions: botanical fidelity versus ornamental folds; deliberately different sectional densities versus a smoother rhythm; how much shading stays readable at actual size and with the chosen bit.
